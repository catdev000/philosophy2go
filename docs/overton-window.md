# Overton Window

:::article
## Joseph Overton
<img src="" 
    alt="Image of Joseph Overton">
Overton formulated the idea of the Overton window to explain the role of think tanks and to persuade potential donors to support policy advocacy. He reasoned that instead of lobbying politicians directly, think tanks should advocate for "unthinkable" or radical ideas to shift public perception; this would make moderate versions of those ideas seem sensible by comparison, eventually allowing them to pass as policy.
:::

## Explaination

    alt="Graphical explaination of Overton Window">
Shifting of the Overton Window  
The sayable is shifted into one side. What is policy moves slowly becomes a radical topic. What was once radical becomes policy. This can be caused by a general shift of opinion in society.  
Widening of the Overton Window  
The sayable is widened in both or one side. What was radical becomes popular policy.  
Shrinking of the Overton Window  
The sayable is shrunk in both or one side. What was popular becomes radical.
:::

## Examples

### Critique on Religion
For most of human history, open criticism of religious doctrine was considered unthinkable — and in many societies, it was criminal. Blasphemy and heresy carried penalties ranging from imprisonment to execution. The Enlightenment (17th–18th century) began the long shift: thinkers such as Voltaire and David Hume challenged religious authority in public, and the gradual separation of church and state made secular critique a political norm.

### Homosexuality
In the 1970s, homosexuality was still criminalized in many Western countries and public discussion was largely taboo — the idea of legal same-sex marriage was unthinkable. Through decriminalization (1980s–90s), the introduction of civil unions (initially a "radical" proposal), and sustained advocacy, the window shifted steadily.

### Shift between pacifism and militarism
This is a bidirectional shift: the window moves toward militarism in times of perceived threat and back toward pacifism after conflicts end.
